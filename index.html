AI Security & Assurance for Healthcare and Critical Infrastructure

I build detection methods and tools for identifying data poisoning, backdoor attacks, and adversarial vulnerabilities in machine learning systems deployed in healthcare and other critical infrastructure sectors — and work toward making that kind of technical verification as routine as the governance frameworks (NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001) that already ask for it.

About

I'm a graduate researcher in Artificial Intelligence at the University of the Cumberlands, based in Fort Worth, Texas. My work focuses on the security and trustworthiness of AI systems deployed in healthcare and other critical infrastructure, with an emphasis on detecting data poisoning, backdoor attacks, and adversarial vulnerabilities in deployed machine learning models. I'm building open-source tools aimed at making independent AI security assurance practical for organizations that can't rely on governance policy alone.

This site collects my published writing, research, and open-source projects in this area as the work develops.

Currently

  • Writing on the gap between AI governance frameworks and technical security verification in healthcare AI.
  • Developing an open-source detection tool for data poisoning and backdoor attacks in ML classification pipelines.
  • Building toward a technical assurance framework mapped to NIST AI RMF and MITRE ATLAS.
